# Human Aging: A Bioinformatics Introduction

## About the Project

This project explores some of the biological processes involved in human aging and the genes associated with them. We are using scientific literature and public biological databases to collect and organize information about these topics.

The idea is to build a starting point for understanding the molecular basis of aging and, as the project develops, work with biological data and bioinformatics tools.

## Topics of Interest

We are starting with three biological processes:

- **Cellular senescence:** the process in which cells stop dividing but remain metabolically active.
- **DNA damage and repair:** the mechanisms involved in detecting and repairing damage to genetic material.
- **Telomere maintenance:** the processes that protect chromosome ends and help regulate cellular lifespan.

## Genes Selected

We selected three genes as an initial reference for exploring these processes:

- **CDKN2A:** associated with cell cycle regulation and cellular senescence.
- **TP53:** involved in cellular responses to DNA damage.
- **TERT:** encodes the catalytic subunit of telomerase, an enzyme involved in telomere maintenance.

This initial selection may change as we review more information and develop the project.

## Project Structure

- `data/`: datasets and biological information.
- `results/`: outputs from future analyses.
- `documentation/`: descriptions of the biological processes under study.
- `references/`: databases and scientific sources.
- `notebooks/`: notebooks for data exploration and analysis.

## Current Status

The repository and its initial structure are in place. The first biological topics and candidate genes have also been selected.

Data collection and bioinformatics analyses will be developed as the project progresses.
